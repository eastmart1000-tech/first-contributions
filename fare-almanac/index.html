Fare Almanac

Compare airlines on any route, domestic or international, and find the cheapest dates to fly and the best time to book.

Fares here are modeled estimates, not live quotes. They come from typical airline pricing patterns: distance, airline price tier, seasonal demand, US holidays, day of week, how far ahead you book, and checked-bag fees. Use them to pick dates, then press a booking link to confirm the live price.

How the estimates work

Base fare. Great-circle miles between the airports set the base price. Short domestic hops cost more per mile than long-haul flights. Smaller airports like OKC and TUL carry a premium.

Airlines. Each carrier has a price tier (legacy, low-cost, ultra-low-cost, long-haul budget) and a real hub network. That network decides whether a route is nonstop or connects, and through which hub.

When you fly. Seasonal demand at the destination, US holidays (Thanksgiving, Christmas, spring break, July 4th), Lunar New Year, and weekday pricing: Tuesday and Wednesday departures usually cost least.

When you book. Prices assume you book today. Domestic fares bottom out about 1 to 3 months ahead, international about 2 to 6 months. Fares rise sharply in the last 3 weeks.
